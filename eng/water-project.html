01   Overview

A single platform for water safety information across Taiwan

Before this platform existed, there was no centralized place for the public to access water activity safety information in Taiwan. Data about beach conditions, river hazards, and activity advisories was scattered across different government departments with no unified entry point.

The National Fire Agency commissioned this project to build an integrated, publicly accessible platform — designed for all citizens regardless of age or digital literacy — consolidating water safety data into one clear, trustworthy service.

02   Problem Definition

Information existed — but no one could find it

Citizens planning water activities had no reliable, consolidated resource to check safety conditions. Information from county governments, fire departments, and meteorological agencies existed in silos, requiring users to navigate multiple official websites — a friction-heavy experience that most people simply gave up on.

The core challenge was both an information architecture problem and an accessibility problem: how to surface safety-critical information clearly and quickly to a general public audience with varying levels of digital fluency.

04   Design Process

From stakeholder interviews to two prototype versions

The design process started with structured requirements interviews with county government officers to understand what information was most operationally critical. This shaped the information architecture before any visual work began.

1
Stakeholder Requirements Interviews
Interviewed county government officers (縣市承辦) to understand what safety information they needed to surface, what data was available, and what constraints existed at the government-data level. This defined the scope and content hierarchy.
2
Information Architecture & User Flow
Mapped out the full user flow and site structure — how a citizen would navigate from landing page to finding specific water safety data for their intended location and activity type.
3
Figma Prototype v1
Built the first low-to-mid fidelity prototype in Figma. Focused on layout logic, content hierarchy, and navigation patterns. Validated internally and with stakeholders.
4
Figma Prototype v2
Iterated based on feedback — refined visual design, accessibility decisions (contrast ratios, color system), and interaction patterns. This version served as the direct handoff reference for frontend development.
5
Component Design & Frontend Build
Designed and built each UI component from scratch in Vanilla JS, integrating ECharts for data visualization. Developed both light and dark theme variants throughout.

05   Visual Design

Accessibility-first color system, built for everyone

Because this is a government public service used by all age groups, the visual design was constrained by accessibility requirements from the start — not retrofitted in. Color choices were driven by contrast ratio compliance (WCAG AA minimum), not aesthetic preference alone.

Water as a domain naturally informed the palette direction: deep navy for the dark theme, light blue for the light theme, with high-contrast white and near-black text for readability in both modes.

Accessibility Principle

Both light and dark themes were designed with contrast ratios verified against WCAG AA standards. The color system avoids hues that cause issues for common types of color vision deficiency, and avoids decorative color that could obscure hierarchy or meaning.

06   Engineering

Vanilla JS with ECharts — keeping the stack lean for a government project

Given the government context and long-term maintenance requirements, the frontend was built in Vanilla JS without a heavy framework dependency. This reduces the risk of dependency rot and makes the codebase accessible to future maintainers who may not be framework specialists.

ECharts was chosen for data visualization due to its robust handling of complex chart types, good accessibility support, and Chinese-language documentation ecosystem relevant to the maintenance team.
